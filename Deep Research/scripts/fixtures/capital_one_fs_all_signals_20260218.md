# Executive Summary

In the past six months, Capital One has navigated significant regulatory and risk management developments. The bank completed its acquisition of Discover Financial Services in May 2025, the largest U.S. banking merger in six years, propelling Capital One to become the eighth-largest bank in the country. Regulatory approvals for this deal came with strict conditions and enforcement actions, including fines and mandated remediation plans related to Discover's prior compliance issues.

Meanwhile, broader regulatory changes have been unfolding: the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency proposed raising the asset threshold for its Heightened Standards from $50 billion to $700 billion, reflecting a deregulatory shift that could ease compliance burdens for banks like Capital One. At the same time, federal banking agencies signaled a refocus of supervision toward material financial risks and possible revision of longstanding guidance such as SR 11-7.

Capital One also faced regulatory scrutiny and legal actions: a high-profile consumer protection lawsuit over deposit account practices that led to a $425 million settlement for customers, and an ongoing dispute with the FDIC regarding the bank's share of a special deposit insurance assessment following 2023 bank failures. On the risk governance front, leadership has remained largely stable, but the bank did appoint a new Business Chief Risk Officer for its global payments network to strengthen risk oversight post-merger.

Stress test results were a bright spot: Capital One's 2025 CCAR results were robust, yielding a reduced Stress Capital Buffer of 4.5% (down from 5.5% the prior year) effective Q4 2025. Additionally, the current regulatory environment has seen fewer large-bank BSA/AML penalties, but oversight bodies continue to identify AML program deficiencies across the market, underscoring that institutions must not grow complacent.

## Key Signal Findings in Financial Services (Capital One | Last 180 Days)

### Consent Orders and Enforcement Actions

M&A-related enforcement: The Federal Reserve and FDIC issued significant enforcement actions in conjunction with approving Capital One's acquisition of Discover. On April 18, 2025, the Fed announced a $100 million consent order against Discover for excessive interchange fees, and the FDIC amended a 2023 consent order adding a $150 million penalty and requiring at least $1.225 billion in restitution to affected merchants. These actions addressed deficiencies in consumer compliance and interchange fee practices over a multi-year period and were coordinated with merger approval.

The OCC, as a condition of merger approval, required Capital One to submit a detailed remediation plan within 120 days of deal closing (mid-September 2025 timeline) to address root causes of outstanding enforcement issues at Discover and ensure sustainable corrective actions and harm remediation.

Consumer compliance legal action: Capital One's deposit practices drew scrutiny. Public reporting described allegations that legacy 360 Savings customers were not sufficiently informed about higher-rate alternatives. Coverage documented a revised settlement framework with substantial restitution and rate-related relief for affected customers.

FDIC special assessment dispute: In September 2025, Capital One sued the FDIC over special-assessment calculations tied to uninsured deposits after 2023 bank failures. In November 2025, the FDIC countersued over alleged underpayment. This dispute highlights sensitivity around regulatory reporting precision and supervisory interpretation.

### Regulatory Compliance Deadlines and Change Signals

Discover remediation plan deadline: The OCC condition set a concrete post-close deliverable timeline for remediation planning and execution tracking.

Evolving requirements: The OCC's Heightened Standards proposal and Federal Reserve stress-capital discussions indicate a shifting regulatory architecture. Banks are expected to remain disciplined on governance, evidence quality, and implementation controls through transitional periods.

### Model Risk Management (SR 11-7) Context

Regulatory commentary suggested potential recalibration in model risk guidance while preserving core expectations for governance, validation, and documentation rigor. Public evidence in this period did not show a clear Capital One model-risk finding, but it reinforced readiness requirements for changing supervisory posture.

### Leadership Changes (CRO/CFO/CCO)

CFO and enterprise risk leadership remained stable through the period, while public reporting highlighted appointment of a Business Chief Risk Officer for the new Global Payments Network. This indicates governance alignment as strategic payments capabilities expand.

### Stress Test (CCAR/DFAST) Developments

Capital One's stress-capital posture improved in the 2025 cycle with lower SCB requirements. Public evidence did not indicate a major objection outcome in the period, but stress-testing governance remains an active strategic-control area given annual cycle pressure and rule-design evolution.

### AML/BSA Compliance Context

Market-wide enforcement cadence softened for major-bank penalties in 2025, but supervisory messaging and selected enforcement actions continue to emphasize AML governance, control effectiveness, and board oversight expectations.

### CECL Implementation Context

Public accounting updates in late 2025 described CECL changes to address acquisition-related double counting. This is strategically relevant to post-merger capital and allowance governance.
